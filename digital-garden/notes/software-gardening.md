Software gardening is the continuous work of keeping a codebase healthy, understandable, and easy to change.

Agents can complete individual tasks while making the overall codebase worse through duplication, tangled dependencies, weak abstractions, misleading tests, unnecessary complexity, and temporary workarounds that become permanent.

The gardener observes the flow of changes, detects recurring smells, improves boundaries, strengthens feedback, removes obsolete code, reviews automated changes, and improves the environment in which future agents work.

The goal is not only to build software. It is to maintain the software factory that builds software.
