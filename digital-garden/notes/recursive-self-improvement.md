Here's the scary feedback loop: AI gets good enough to do AI research. Then that AI-improved AI is even better at doing AI research. Repeat. Each cycle is faster than the last, and at some point humans can't meaningfully verify what changed between versions.

This is [[Recursive Self-Improvement]], and it's basically the [[Scaling Thesis]] on steroids. Instead of humans throwing more compute at the problem, the AI itself figures out how to use compute better — or invents entirely new approaches. The progress compounds.

The weird part Pachocki highlights: the risk isn't that RSI *fails*. It's that it *succeeds*. If AI can improve itself faster than we can understand the improvements, we become spectators to our own technology. We built the first domino, but we didn't choose how the rest fall.

But here's the tension that makes this genuinely hard — RSI isn't just a threat. It might be *necessary*. Cybersecurity already needs AI defending against AI. And solving [[Value Alignment]] itself might require intelligence beyond what humans alone can muster. So you can't just say "don't do RSI" — you need it for defense while it simultaneously creates the offense.

This is why [[Chain-of-Thought Monitoring]] matters so much right now. It's one of the few tools we have to peek inside the loop while it's still slow enough to peek.
