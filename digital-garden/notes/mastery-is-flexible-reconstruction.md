You have not fully learned an idea when you can only repeat it in the order in which it was presented.

A stronger test is whether you can:

- retrieve it without looking at the source,
- explain it forward and backward,
- connect each claim to the others,
- apply it to a new example,
- state the strongest opposing case,
- identify what evidence would change your mind.

This separates familiarity from usable understanding. Recognition says, “I have seen this before.” Reconstruction asks, “Can I generate and use it without the original path?”

A general learning loop is:

1. **Understand fairly** — reconstruct the strongest version before evaluating it.
2. **Retrieve flexibly** — close the source and reproduce the idea in altered orders.
3. **Trace the warrant** — follow important claims back to their evidence.
4. **Challenge the model** — construct the strongest objections and invite an independent opponent.
5. **Attempt application** — solve a difficult problem before studying the standard technique.
6. **Revise** — record what failed, what changed, and what remains uncertain.

The point is not to memorize more aggressively. It is to build a dependency graph in which each idea can be reached from multiple directions.

This turns learning into a feedback loop: retrieval and application expose gaps; objections expose blind spots; evidence checks expose unsupported assumptions.
