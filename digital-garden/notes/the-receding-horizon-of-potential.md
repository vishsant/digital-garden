> "There is no peace for the man who has seen what he could become."
>
> — commonly attributed to Epictetus, but not found in the *Discourses* or *Enchiridion*

## The claim

Once you glimpse what you're capable of, you lose the ability to sit comfortably where you are. Ignorance of your own potential is a shelter; seeing destroys it. And the restlessness that follows is *permanent* — not "less peace," but **no** peace.

## Why the gap never closes

The vision of "what you could become" isn't a fixed destination. It's a **horizon** — it recedes at exactly the rate you approach it.

A beginner guitarist envisions playing a song cleanly. The moment they can, they now *hear* phrasing, dynamics, feel — a whole layer that was invisible before. The gap didn't close. It widened, because the capacity to see grows alongside the capacity to do.

This is what makes the restlessness structural rather than motivational. It's not "I'm not there yet." It's that "there" keeps moving.

## The tone is neutral

The quote isn't a warning ("don't look"), a celebration ("you're special for seeing"), or a lament ("you lost something"). It's a plain description — stating a consequence the way you'd state a law of physics.

*If you have seen, then peace is not available.*

The neutrality is what gives it force. It leaves you to decide whether the trade was worth it.

## Where the claim breaks

The quote treats seeing the gap and being at peace as **mutually exclusive**. But that's an assumption, not a law.

Master craftsmen, seasoned artists, long-practicing monks — people exist who see vast distances ahead and still sit comfortably where they are. They hold both at once: clear sight of the horizon *and* genuine peace in the present step.

What the quote actually captures is a **specific relationship** to the gap — one where distance feels like a debt you owe. But there's an alternative: the gap is just *interesting*. The horizon moves, and that's not a burden — it's the reason the craft stays alive.

**Peace doesn't require the journey to be over. It requires you to not be at war with where you currently stand.**
